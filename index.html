Globalisation

Comprehensive Revision Sheet — Activity of Integration Preparation

Definition & Key Terms
▾
Globalisation is the process by which the world is becoming increasingly interconnected as a result of massively increased trade and cultural exchange. It involves the integration of economies, cultures, markets, and policies around the world.
Key Terms to Know
Multilateral Corporations (MNCs) Trade Blocs (EAC, NAFTA) Balance of Payments NGOs CBOs Foreign Aid Neo-colonialism Cultural Imperialism Containerization Financial Liberalization Intra-community Trade Offshoring
⚙️
Factors Promoting Globalisation in East Africa / Uganda
▾
  • Digital revolution & technology — Internet reduced communication costs; easy access to goods and services globally
  • Containerization — Cut cost of ocean shipping; made bulk transport efficient
  • Transnational & multilateral corporations — Need for lower labour costs abroad promoted globalisation
  • Less protectionism & tariff-free trade zones — Opened borders to more trade
  • Capitalistic strategies & profit maximization — Companies seek new markets globally
  • Electronic payment systems — e-wallets, mobile pay, pre-pay promoted cross-border transactions
  • Increased capital mobility — Global credit markets promoted investment across borders
  • Increase in free trade — Promoted globalisation
  • Agents of globalisation — UNO, AU, EEC led growth of globalisation
  • International economic integration — Trade blocs like NAFTA (US, Mexico, Canada) and EAC (East Africa) reduced border barriers
  • Growth of NGOs and CBOs — Promoted globalisation in Uganda
  • Socio-cultural convergence — Social media and online news; people adopting global cultures (e.g. fashion)
  • Global education providers — Online learning; internationally accepted qualifications
  • Cross-border political influence — China's long-term loans to East African countries
  • Financial liberalization — Easy exchange of money to global currencies (US dollar, British pound)
  • Intense competition among firms — Pushing businesses to find new global markets
💡 Exam Tip
The conclusion in your notes summarises: digital revolution; international economic integration; socio-cultural convergence; global education; cross-border political influence; financial liberalization; and intense competition — memorise these as the headline drivers.

Advantages / Merits of Globalisation
▾
  • Wider talent pool — Companies hire from all over the world; better efficiency and production
  • Cultural diversity & workforce — Multiple cultural backgrounds produce better ideas
  • New and larger markets — Increased specialisation opportunities
  • Higher living standards — Health and welfare achievements improved
  • Free trade reduces barriers — Tariffs lowered; better balance of trade
  • Competition drives down prices — Better quality goods and living standards
  • Capital and technology for developing countries — Poor countries get resources to develop
  • Democracy and human rights flourish — Better global governance conditions created
  • Better information between countries — Even countries with nothing in common exchange ideas
  • Cultural infusion — Countries learn each other's cultures; greater tolerance
  • Quick dissemination of information — Faster, better means of travel and communication
  • Labour mobility — Workers market their skills across countries
  • Technology sharing — Leads to progress especially in young nations
  • Employment opportunities — Created when multilateral companies invest in developing countries
  • Free trade agreements — e.g. NAFTA; resource pooling like joint space station construction
  • Reduced tyranny and oppression — Especially in poor young nations under constant global check
  • Eliminates trade inefficiencies — Through elimination of tariffs etc.
  • Attracts foreign capital and technology — Improved quality of production
  • Peaceful relations — Countries put aside bitter past experiences
  • Environmental conservation — Global cooperation on environment
  • Balanced development of world economies
  • Industrial development in East Africa — Free flow of technology
  • Promotes global legal regimes
  • Global peace through intervention where necessary
  • Improved governance — Better labour protection through competitive pressures
❌
Disadvantages / Demerits of Globalisation
▾
  • Reduced earnings for low-skilled workers — Especially in developing countries
  • Social injustice — MNCs exploit workers; slave labour wages; poor working conditions
  • Food price inflation (Agflation) — Strong demand has led to steep inflation affecting poor countries
  • Vulnerability to external economic shocks — Financial crisis in USA can affect Uganda
  • Environmental damage — Land degradation, deforestation, loss of biodiversity
  • Trade imbalances — Poor countries like Uganda disadvantaged
  • Foreign multinationals influence politics — In some parts of the world
  • Intellectual property theft — Products easily replicated in other countries
  • Internal communication challenges — When people don't work in same location
  • Rising inequalities — Rich countries richer, poor countries poorer
  • Jobs transferred to low-cost countries (offshoring) — Local jobs lost
  • Salary cuts in developed countries — Threat of export of jobs
  • MNCs exploit tax havens — Avoid paying taxes
  • Spread of deadly diseases — HIV/AIDS, COVID-19 spread through global travel
  • Social welfare (safety nets) under pressure — Due to job losses
  • Loss of cultural identities — Cultural imperialism
  • Environmental damage — Greenhouse gas emissions
  • Superiority of bigger nations over smaller ones
  • Neo-colonialism — And associated evils
  • More imports than exports — Growing trade deficit and balance of payment issues
  • Small scale industries wiped out — By multilateral companies
